# HRP conversation-preparation example (illustrative, not validated market research)

## Starting idea
“An AI agent to help HR prepare for an employee conversation.”

## Reframing from exploratory dialogue
- Reported SHRM mentor observation: some HR practitioners may default to transaction-oriented conflict resolution rather than exploratory dialogue. This is a practitioner's report, not measured prevalence.
- Proposed distribution: free benefit for paid HRP members. This is a planned channel, not proven engagement or retention.
- Intended outcome: clarify facts, relationships, stakes, assumptions and conversational goals; not simply generate scripts.
- Immediate job: prepare for a difficult conversation; professional growth is a possible longer-term effect.

## Current best hypothesis
HRP members may value a confidential, professionally structured pre-conversation thinking partner.

## Rival hypotheses
- A short guided worksheet or mentor session achieves the same value.
- HR members prefer fast phrasing assistance, not 10–20 minutes of reflection.
- Trust/privacy constraints prevent real use.
- Value is episodic and does not improve membership renewal.

## Example checkpoint
**Changed:** from generic HR agent to member benefit for HR-led sensitive conversations.
**Known:** sponsor's intent and reported expert observation.
**Unknown:** frequency, real use, comparative value, data willingness, retention effect.
**Disconfirming test:** observe a small number of genuine users preparing for real conversations, comparing a simple worksheet / general assistant with structured questioning; collect behavior and outcome quality, not only satisfaction.
**Next:** validate problem and engagement before building an agent.

No claim in this example is an actual user study.
